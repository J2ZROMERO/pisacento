# Product requirements

## Purpose

Help a beginner connect listening, counting and weight changes in Salsa and Bachata. Instrument mixing alone is not the intended distinction: explain what the learner should do, why it helps and what source supports it.

## Required experience

1. Choose Salsa or Bachata from two large opening cards.
2. Choose the available style/context and individual/partner mode.
3. See a progressive route, one lesson at a time, with beginner explanations.
4. Practise with a single feet view and an explicit role or first-foot convention.
5. Inspect counts and distinguish musical events from dance supports.
6. Open sources with their scope and limitations.

Use mobile-first layouts, accessible native controls, visible focus and collapsible explanations. Keep advanced and historical resources separate from the main route.

## Active scope

- Salsa: the evidence allowlist exposes On1 and New York On2. Archived data can contain additional styles or exercises; that does not make them approved UI content.
- Salsa music: sourced clave patterns and partial bell/conga references, plus an explicitly electronic metronome. Partial references are not complete instrumental parts.
- Bachata: six supports and two taps in an eight-count, practised in place. Tap on 4/8 does not fully transfer weight.
- Bachata variants: traditional/Dominican, modern/social, sensual, with different context and sources. The same foundation exercise is retained.
- Bachata listening: five original published instrument examples, with counted alternatives for three instruments. Streaming is independent of local feet timing.

## Non-goals and claims to avoid

Do not promise universal song transcription, automatic identification of every instrument, perfect detection of the first beat, universal dance conventions, official certification of the app, or automatic scoring of someone's dancing. Institutional heritage recognition and a teacher's technical convention support different kinds of claims.

## Future validation

The hypothesis is that beginners will understand timing with less confusion. It has not been established by a user study. Validate with learners and qualified teachers before claiming better learning outcomes.
